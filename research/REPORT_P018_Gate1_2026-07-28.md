## 2. Gate 1R-A — the placebo. **KILL.**

Placebo construction, exactly as pre-registered: same `_fade_spec` arithmetic,
same 0.5 edge fraction, same 240 s life, same taper, same strictly-through
fill rule, same series-aware maker fee, same settlement — rested at ticks
that are **≥ 600 s from any observed event** and where `|fair − mid| ≥ 0.015`,

One difference: no surprising event just happened.

| arm | fills | games | net ¢/ct | 95% CI | z |

| **fade** | 5,676 | 91 | **+9.09** | [+5.53, +12.75] | 4.91 |

**Pre-registered condition applied mechanically:** placebo is **211%** of the
fade (≥ 50%) and the CIs overlap → **KILL**.

**Stated honestly, because it cuts the other way:** the placebo's own CI is
enormous and it is *not* significantly positive (z = 1.94). This is not
"non-event quoting is better." It is **"the two arms are indistinguishable,
and if anything the surprise arm is worse."** The KILL condition was written
around failure-to-separate for exactly this reason, and it fires on the
strongest possible version of that failure: the point estimate goes the wrong
way.

## 4. Gate 1R-C — dose-response with quote distance held fixed. **KILL.**

Fills stratified into terciles of model-predicted edge; within each, bucketed
by surprise. **This is the test the original gate #1 should have been.**

### As the strategy actually quotes (`surprise_hi = 0.06`)

| predicted-edge stratum | surprise [0.06, 0.12) | surprise [0.12, 1.01) | slope |
|---|---:|---:|---|
| 1 — up to +3.53 ¢ | −0.67 ¢ | −0.18 ¢ | flat (CIs overlap entirely) |
| 2 — +3.53 to +13.42 ¢ | −1.78 ¢ | **−3.90 ¢** | **negative** |
| 3 — above +13.42 ¢ | **+30.24 ¢** | **+30.67 ¢** | flat |

**Pre-registered condition:** *slope flat or negative in every stratum →

**And look at the column, not the row.** The entire edge is stratum 3. Strata
1 and 2 — two thirds of all fills — are **at or below zero**. The +9.09 ¢
headline is one third of the sample earning +30 ¢ and the rest earning
nothing.

## 5. What the edge actually is — a hypothesis, labelled as one

Predicted edge is `0.5 × |fair − mid|`, so stratum 3's threshold of +11.45 ¢
means the model and the market **disagree by ≥ 23 cents**. A 23-cent
disagreement between a win-probability model and a live Kalshi mid on an MLB
game is not a market inefficiency anyone can harvest; it is far more likely
one of:

* **A stale mid.** `book_capture` runs on a **60-second** cadence, so the
  recorded `mid` can be a minute old while the prints replayed against it are
  current. Fading a minute-old price with a model built from fresh
  play-by-play is a look-ahead, not an edge.
* **A broken pregame anchor.** `replay_market` sets
  `pregame = mid if mid is not None else 0.54` from the **first observed
  tick**. If capture starts mid-game — which the 33.9% lowest-volume-first
  drop makes routine — the anchor is a mid-game price used as a pregame
  prior, and `home_win_probability` is miscalibrated for that entire market.
* **Genuine model error** at extreme states.

**This is not tested here and I am not claiming it.** It is the obvious next
question, and it is a question about the *model*, not about surprise. Which is
the point: **1R-B already showed the gate's real dependency is model
calibration, and §5 is what that dependency looks like when you push on it.**

## 6. Corrections to my own pre-registration and rule

**Two, both against my own prediction, both disclosed.**

1. **`P018_DECISION_RULE.md` §2 predicted the CI would WIDEN by ≈1.39× once
   clustering moved from ticker to game. It NARROWED, by about 7%.**

   | clustering | 95% CI | width |
   |---|---|---:|
   | ticker (published, same 5,676 fills) | [+5.33, +13.10] | 7.77 ¢ |
   | **game (correct)** | [+5.53, +12.75] | 7.22 ¢ |

   The unit correction stands — 240 tickers really are 124 games, and the two
   markets of a game really are one observation. But my variance reasoning was
   wrong: because the two tickers of a game are **anti-correlated** (one team
   wins), pooling them into one cluster lets their P&L partially offset, which
   *reduces* between-cluster variance rather than increasing it. The
   `√(240/124)` arithmetic assumed positively-correlated or independent
   within-cluster observations. §2 has been corrected in place.

   **It does not change any verdict** — the correction cuts *in favour of* the
   headline, and the headline is killed by the mechanism tests regardless.

2. **P-018 does not appear in `scripts/check_gate_instrumentation.py`'s output
   at all.** It is registered `stage: build`, `blocked_on: backtest`, with
   **no `gate` block** — deliberately, so throughput cannot project a date for
   a pod with no decision rule. The standard only checks pods that *have* a
   gate, so **the standard silently skips it**. That is a real limitation of
   the standard, reported not fixed: *a pod with no gate block passes by
   absence.* Adding a gate block for a pod that has just failed its kill gate
   would be the wrong fix, so nothing was added.
