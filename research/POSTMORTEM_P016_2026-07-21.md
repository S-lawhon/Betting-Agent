This is a paper pilot that did its job: it disproved a hypothesis cheaply, on a
pre-registered rule, and told us exactly *why* it failed. That is a successful
experiment with a negative result — not a failure of process.

---

## 1. What P-016 was

A live in-play market maker on Kalshi MLB game-winner markets. It rested a bid
and an ask around a fair-value anchor during innings 1–8, earned the spread
when public flow crossed its quotes, and held to settlement. Paper throughout.

The economic thesis: *quote tightly around a good anchor, collect the bid-ask
spread, and the spread exceeds what you lose being picked off.*

## 2. The gate, and why it was built that way

Pre-registered before deployment:

> ≥500 fills **AND** positive fee-adjusted +5m markout **AND** robust to
> excluding the best single day → else kill.

- **Metric was markout, not P&L.** Markout asks: 5 minutes after a fill, where
  did fair value move? It isolates maker skill from settlement luck. A separate
  analysis this cycle
  ([MARKOUT_VS_SETTLEMENT_2026-07-20.md](../live_agent_research/MARKOUT_VS_SETTLEMENT_2026-07-20.md))
  confirmed markout is the right metric here: unbiased, ~10× more
  sample-efficient than settlement P&L, and the settlement sample (5 games) was
  far too small to gate on.
- **The ex-best-day clause** existed precisely so a thin, favourable sample
  couldn't buy the pod more time.

## 3. What the final sample showed

| Measure | Value |
|---|---|
| Fills | 814 / 500 |
| Spread capture at fill | **+4.08¢** (positive) |
| +1m markout | −2.26¢ |
| **+5m markout (the gate)** | **−1.29¢ / −$107.59 net** |
| +15m markout | −1.94¢ |
| Settled realized P&L | +$33.32 (160/814 fills, luck not skill) |

Verified with the project's own `scripts.maker_report` on the droplet, not a
hand computation.

**The result in two numbers: spread capture +4.08¢, markout −1.29¢.** The pod
*earned* the spread. It then lost more than all of it to adverse selection. The
gate failed on the headline arm — markout was negative, so the ex-best-day
robustness check was never even reached.

## 4. Why it failed — diagnosed, not guessed

Tier 2's offline evaluator measured the mechanism directly:

> Fills within 15 seconds of an observed game-state change markout **−8.78¢**,
> versus **−0.96¢** outside that window.

This is the Glosten–Milgrom pick-off, visible in the tape. When a pitch is
thrown or a run scores, the fair value moves, and the counterparties who cross
your quote in the next few seconds are the ones who saw it first. Your anchor
updates on the *next* cycle; their trade lands on *this* one. Market-anchored
quoting has structurally no defence against this — the anchor is always a step
behind the flow that picks it off.

The +4.08¢ spread is real and it is not enough: the informed flow near state
changes costs more than the whole spread earns.

## 5. Lessons — carry these into v2 and beyond

**L1 — Spread capture ≠ profit. Markout is the honest metric.**
A positive spread capture with negative markout is the signature of a maker
being picked off. Any future maker must be judged on markout, and the +4.08¢
here is a standing reminder that "we're earning the spread" is not evidence of
edge.

**L2 — [CORRECTED] The loss is DIFFUSE, not localised — which is worse news.**
The original L2 claimed the pick-off was concentrated in a ±15s window and
therefore defensible. Tested on the full clean tape, that does not hold: the
0–5s spike is real but is ~2% of volume, while 62% of fills sit >60s from any
state change and still markout −2.2¢. A market-anchored maker here bleeds on
*ordinary* fills, not just event-adjacent ones. That means there is no cheap
structural fix (no window to defend), and it points to the strategy lacking edge
rather than lacking a guardrail. **The lesson to carry: verify the shape of a
loss before designing a defence around it — a plausible "concentrated" story
sent an hour of design effort at a window that wasn't where the money leaked.**

**L3 — A market-anchored maker cannot out-run the market it anchors to.** If
the anchor is the market mid, informed flow moves the mid and hits the stale
quote in the same instant. A maker needs either (a) a faster-than-market signal,
or (b) to stop quoting when it knows it is stale — v2 takes route (b), which is
cheaper and needs no forecasting edge.

**L4 — Pre-registration worked exactly as designed.** The result was thin (one
live slate, best-day-heavy) and it would have been easy to rationalise "collect
more data." The ex-best-day clause and the hard markout sign made the call
mechanical. Keep pre-registering, and keep the clauses that defend against
your own future optimism.

**L5 — The paper tape only records fills that crossed the champion's quotes.**
This bounded what Tier 2 could evaluate (wider arms are measurable, tighter ones
are not — their fills were never recorded). It is not a flaw, but it shapes what
questions the existing data can answer, and v2's evaluation plan must account
for it rather than assume the tape can measure any arm.

**L6 — Process notes worth keeping.**
- The gate metric lives in `maker_fills.jsonl` as separate markout records, not
  as a field on the fill — use `scripts.maker_report`, don't hand-roll it.
- First-night data was anchor-contaminated (a restart re-anchored live books,
  producing 484 phantom one-way fills in 45 min); it was archived to
  `contaminated_2026-07-20/` and excluded via `exclude_before`. Any v2 restart
  needs the same anchor-reset guard, or it will manufacture the same artifact.
- The kill switch (`touch data/KILL_MAKER`) pulls quotes within one cycle but
  fills still trickle for one more cycle from prints crossing already-resting
  quotes. That is correct, not a stuck kill — verify by checking that no new
  QUOTE records post after the final PULL, not by expecting fills to stop
  instantly.

## 6. What was NOT the problem — don't over-correct

- **Not the spread.** +4.08¢ capture is healthy. Widening it further would trade
  fill rate for spread and does not touch the adverse-selection loss.
- **Not fill volume.** 814 fills is ample; the pilot was well-powered.
- **Not the fee model.** P-016 used the correct maker rate (verified: it passes
  no `series_ticker`, hitting the 0.0175 fallback for KXMLBGAME, which does
  charge). The MLB-prop fee misclassification fixed this cycle never touched it.
- **Not settlement/realized P&L.** The +$33.32 realized is 160 lucky fills and
  is not evidence of anything; do not let it argue for a reprieve.
